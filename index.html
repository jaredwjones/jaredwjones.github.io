I am a PhD candidate in the Department of Philosophy at Columbia University. I work on German Idealism, especially Kant and Hegel, and on 19th- and 20th-century European philosophy.

My dissertation, Structures of Subjectivity in Hegel’s Logic, reconstructs Hegel’s account of what makes us thinkers and doers. My other work currently focuses on related issues in metaphysics and philosophy of mind in German Idealism.

Before Columbia, I studied philosophy and mathematics at the College of William & Mary. I co-organize the New York German Idealism Workshop.

Dissertation

Structures of Subjectivity in Hegel’s Logic
Committee: Frederick Neuhouser (advisor), Axel Honneth, Wolfgang Mann

My dissertation is about Hegel’s account of subjectivity in his Science of Logic. I argue that the Logic seeks to show that knowledge and practical agency are only possible for a being with a certain metaphysical structure: the structure of a subject. On this view, the subject is a living thing that represents objects with two sorts of reflexivity, a sensitivity to its own representing and to its own actions. The account that emerges situates the subject in the world, like Hegel’s realist predecessors, while keeping his idealist predecessors’ insights into the autonomy of thinking and willing.
