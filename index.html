Hi! I'm a PhD candidate in the Department of Philosophy at Columbia University. I work on German Idealism, especially Kant and Hegel, and on 19th- and 20th-Century European Philosophy.

My dissertation, Structures of Subjectivity in Hegel’s Logic, reconstructs Hegel’s account of subjectivity, of what makes us thinkers and doers. My other work currently focuses on related issues in metaphysics and philosophy of mind in German Idealism. I co-organize the New York German Idealism Workshop.

I studied philosophy and mathematics at the College of William & Mary, where I received my BA in 2022.

Dissertation

Structures of Subjectivity in Hegel’s Logic
Committee: Frederick Neuhouser (advisor), Axel Honneth, Wolfgang Mann

My dissertation is about Hegel’s account of subjectivity in his Science of Logic. I argue that the Logic seeks to show that knowledge and practical agency are activities only possible for a being with a certain metaphysical structure: the structure of a subject. On Hegel’s account, we are to interpret the subject as a living thing that represents objects with two sorts of reflexivity: one by which I relate to myself as a thinker and knower, another by which I relate to myself as a free agent. I present his account of what it means for us to be things, living, and reflexive beings as the culmination of attempts to answer general philosophical questions related to subjectivity. Can subjects be “things” at all? What would they have to be like to be governed by norms? How would they have to represent to know? How must they relate to their own actions? The account that emerges situates the subject in the world (like Hegel’s realist predecessors), while retaining key insights of his idealist predecessors on the autonomy of thinking and willing.

Works-in-Progress

The Role of the Explanatory Ought in Kant's Theory of Teleology

Theories of function often argue that claims about something’s function or purpose can be formulated in causal terms, and a popular interpretation of Kant’s theory of teleology holds he takes the same approach. In this paper, I argue that his theory instead presents a powerful alternative to it. On his account, purposes are irreducibly normative and yet still have explanatory implications. To explain something teleologically is to explain how it is from how it ought to be, which yields a model of teleological explanation that proceeds from both normative teleological grounds and ordinary causal grounds. Even so, Kant argues that his view doesn’t theoretically commit us to positing anything beyond the non-teleological posits of modern science.
